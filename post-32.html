A Letter to the One Who Daydreams

From the brain you keep blaming for everything.

Dear Human,

A letter from the brain you keep blaming for everything.

Can we address the allegations?

Apparently, I am responsible for your inability to stay in the present moment.

Interesting.

Because yesterday, I gave you a perfectly normal Tuesday. Sun was shining, coffee was available, nobody was bothering you.

And what did you do?

You imagined moving to Italy, opening a little bookstore, falling in love with a man who owns a vineyard, having a beautiful kitchen with sunlight coming through the windows and wearing linen dresses.

For FOUR HOURS.

And then you came back to reality, looked at your laundry pile and blamed ME.

Excuse me?

I was simply providing entertainment. You were the one who cast a complete stranger as your fictional husband.

Also, why am I being blamed for conversations you have never had?

You have delivered Oscar-worthy speeches to people who have no idea they are involved in a conflict. You have won arguments against people who haven't even disagreed with you. You have also imagined accidentally bumping into someone from your past, looking exceptionally beautiful, and walking away while they regret everything.

I mean, the production budget in here is insane.

And don't even get me started on your imaginary future.

One minute we're unemployed, the next we're giving an acceptance speech at an award ceremony. One minute we're crying over a minor inconvenience, the next we're living in a beautiful apartment with a wardrobe that would make Pinterest jealous.

I have built approximately 37 different versions of your life.

Have you picked one?

NO.

Instead, you sit there saying, "I daydream too much. My brain is so annoying."

First of all, rude.

Second, have you considered that perhaps reality needs better storytelling?

But honestly, I do have one small request.

Could you occasionally visit the actual life we are living?

There are people to meet, food to eat, places to see and experiences that don't require an imaginary backstory.

We can still have our little cinematic moments. I am not cancelling the Italy fantasy. That vineyard is looking promising.

But perhaps we could also finish the dishes?

Yours creatively, dramatically and with absolutely no intention of shutting up,

Your Brain.

P.S. That imaginary conversation you had in the shower yesterday? I have written a sequel. You're welcome.
