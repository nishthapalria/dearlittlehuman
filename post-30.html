A Letter From Your Tummy

Written with great disappointment, considerable acidity and absolutely no peace.

Dear Madam,

Love, Your Tummy.

I am writing this letter with great disappointment, considerable acidity and absolutely no peace.

WE NEED TO TALK.

What exactly is your problem with normal food?

Every single time we go out, you look at the menu and say, "I will have the spicy one."

The waiter asks, "Are you sure? It's very spicy."

You: "Oh, I can handle it."

CAN YOU?

Because I am the one handling it. And frankly, I did not sign up for this.

Last Tuesday, you ordered extra spicy schezwan noodles. EXTRA. SPICY.

I sent you approximately 17 warning signals.

You ignored all of them.

You even had the audacity to say, "It's not that spicy."

Oh, really? Then why were we drinking water like we'd just crossed the Sahara?

And don't even get me started on your relationship with green chillies.

You don't eat them. You collect them.

Every time someone says, "It's a little spicy," your eyes light up like you've just discovered a new hobby.

Meanwhile, I am down here fighting for my life.

You also have this ridiculous habit of eating spicy food and then immediately following it with something sweet.

As if a spoonful of ice cream is going to undo the decisions of a grown woman.

And the next morning?

You suddenly become very spiritual.

"Please God, let my stomach be okay today."

GIRL. GOD WAS NOT AT THE RESTAURANT. YOU WERE.

I would also like to address your so-called cheat days.

You eat spicy food on Monday, spicy food on Tuesday, and on Wednesday you decide to give me a break by ordering something mildly spicy.

MILDLY?

I am begging you to discover the existence of potatoes.

Listen, I love you. I really do.

But could we occasionally eat something that doesn't require an emergency meeting between your taste buds and my digestive system?

I would appreciate a little rice, some yoghurt, perhaps a nice, peaceful meal.

We don't have to prove anything to a chilli.

We are already impressive people.

With love, indigestion and very limited patience,

Your Tummy.

P.S. The next time you say "Make it extra spicy," I am forwarding your food order to your mother.
